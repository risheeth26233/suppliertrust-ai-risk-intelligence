# SupplierTrust Week 02 — Evidence sources

Research reviewed 20 November 2026. These sources establish the relevance of supplier due diligence; none evaluated or endorses SupplierTrust.

## NIST SP 1326

- **Publication:** *NIST Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management: Due Diligence Assessment Quick-Start Guide*, NIST SP 1326
- **Organization/date:** National Institute of Standards and Technology; final publication 8 July 2026
- **Canonical source:** https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1326/final
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.1326
- **Relevant section:** Abstract, especially scope and definition of due-diligence research.
- **Verified claim:** NIST describes due diligence as researching available, pertinent information about a supplier or product to inform decisions about new acquisitions or existing systems. The guide concerns cybersecurity supply-chain risk management and is scoped to ICT suppliers; it identifies FOCI, provenance, resilience, foundational cyber practices and supply-chain tiers as assessment components.
- **Relationship to the product:** Context for why a structured supplier-information review can be useful. SupplierTrust's demo is not a NIST assessment and does not implement or certify the NIST guide.
- **Qualification:** The guide says due-diligence assessments can apply to any supplier, while the guide itself is scoped to ICT suppliers. Do not present the guide as evidence that every SupplierTrust indicator meets NIST requirements.

## OECD Responsible Business Outlook 2026

- **Publication:** *OECD Responsible Business Outlook 2026: Making Commitments Count*
- **Organization/date:** OECD; 30 June 2026
- **Canonical chapter:** https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-responsible-business-outlook-2026_2b15370f-en/full-report/how-are-companies-implementing-due-diligence-for-responsible-business-conduct_52523408.html
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1787/2b15370f-en
- **Relevant section:** Chapter 2, “Identifying and assessing risks and impacts”; “Few companies are assessing environmental and social risks in their supply chain”; Figure 2.6; Annex A.
- **Verified claim:** The report says 16% of large listed companies report evaluating supplier risk using environmental criteria and 18% using human-rights criteria. It summarizes this as fewer than 20% reporting evaluation against those criteria.
- **Relationship to the product:** The report describes a gap in publicly reported supplier-risk evaluation and gives context for making recorded review evidence visible. It does not measure SupplierTrust's impact.
- **Qualification:** The data concerns the largest 10,000 globally listed companies and public disclosures. The indicators are partial proxies, not a complete measure of due diligence. The film says “report” and “large listed companies”; it does not generalize to all businesses.

## U.S. Treasury OFAC FAQ 5

- **Publication:** “When screening for sanctions, how do I determine if I have a valid match to a name on one of OFAC's lists?” in “Assessing OFAC Name Matches,” FAQ 5
- **Organization/date:** U.S. Department of the Treasury, Office of Foreign Assets Control; live FAQ page reviewed 20 November 2026 (the page does not give a publication date)
- **Canonical source:** https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/topic/1591
- **Relevant section:** Step 3, evaluating the quality of a potential match.
- **Verified claim:** OFAC says many potential screening matches are false positives and recommends comparing the full listing with available information, gathering additional details when needed, and considering the totality of the match.
- **Relationship to the product:** Supports the film's explanation that a name similarity is a lead for review, not a confirmed match. The public demo's screening example is explicitly synthetic.
- **Qualification:** The FAQ discusses OFAC's sanctions program and does not validate the application's synthetic fixture or provide legal advice for every jurisdiction.
